How we research and choose topics

Useful problems, explicit evidence and visible limits.

By Everyday Japan Lab · Reviewed 2026-09-24 · Research and planning, not product certification

Start with a problem

A topic needs a concrete reader decision and a useful output. Novelty alone is insufficient. We screen Japanese maturity, a specific overseas use case, search intent, purchase-stage questions, likely advertiser categories, possible affiliate fit, competition, tool usefulness, source availability and safety. These are separate judgements; we do not disguise them as a precise composite score.

Distinguish kinds of evidence

  • Public statistics: check the date, population and metric. Shipments are not installed households.
  • Official recommendations: show what an agency advises, not how many people follow it.
  • Manufacturer documents: establish a named product’s specification or the company’s own claim. They do not independently prove market size or comparative superiority.
  • Editorial hypotheses: overseas demand, query ideas, competition and commercial fit remain hypotheses until validated with target-market evidence.

Use sources within their scope

We prefer public agencies, standards bodies and original manufacturer documentation. A model-specific installation manual does not create a universal rule. We identify regional differences and review sources when content or tools change. The source register records scope and checked dates.

Make calculators inspectable

Each tool shows units, formulas, assumptions, input bounds and exclusions. Example values are not measured averages. Inputs are validated, changed inputs clear old results, and adverse outcomes such as negative savings remain visible. Calculators support planning rather than certifying health, safety or code compliance.

Publish selectively

The editorial inventory contains 110 screened candidates; it is not a claim that all 110 are proven opportunities or widely adopted in Japan. Only three themes have full guides at this stage. Topics with weak maturity evidence, difficult local verification or high harm potential are held back. We do not publish an article simply to fill a keyword list.

Correct visibly

Corrections should update the relevant page, its source or formula and its reviewed date. Material changes belong in the project change log. Report an issue with the affected page and supporting detail.
