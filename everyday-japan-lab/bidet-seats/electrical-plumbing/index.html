Bidet seat electricity and plumbing: questions for your installer

A shopping checklist for local professional verification, with a simple way to think about running cost.

By Everyday Japan Lab · Reviewed 2026-09-24 · Research and planning, not product certification

Read the rating for the actual market

Find the voltage, frequency, current or power rating and installation manual for the exact model being sold. A similar model name in another country may have a different specification. Ask whether the product is approved and supported in the country where it will be installed.

Use the manual to prepare a question list: Is the supply appropriate? Is the outlet correctly grounded and protected? Is its location acceptable in this bathroom? Does the circuit have the needed capacity? Can the cord reach without violating the instructions? These are local installation questions, not a generic website approval. The reference manual illustrates how product-specific these instructions are. [1]

Check the water connection as a system

Record the shut-off valve type, accessible connection space and the specified pressure range. Have an installer confirm thread compatibility, the permitted supply connection and any required backflow arrangement. Do not force a connector that merely appears to fit. Confirm the manufacturer’s leak-check and maintenance instructions before use.

Estimate operating cost from energy, not peak power

Annual cost = measured or declared annual energy (kWh/year) × your electricity rate (currency/kWh)

For an illustrative calculation only, 100 kWh per year at 0.20 currency units per kWh costs 20 currency units per year. Neither input is a typical bidet consumption or current tariff claim. If a bill has time-of-use prices, a single average price simplifies the calculation.

Do not multiply peak nameplate watts by 24 hours unless the device actually draws that load continuously. Heating and standby behaviour differ. Use a measurement method suitable for the rated load and bathroom installation; do not introduce an unsafe temporary electrical arrangement to obtain a reading.

Decide what remains unresolved

The readiness worksheet distinguishes unknown supply ratings, a known voltage mismatch and unverified outlet or valve conditions. Even a matching voltage label is only one check. Keep physical fit, electrical approval and plumbing approval as separate questions so that a favourable answer to one does not conceal a problem with another.
