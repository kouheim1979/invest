Japanese bidet seats: what to check before buying

A warm-water wash can be useful. The buying decision starts with your toilet, local utilities and the exact seat drawing.

By Everyday Japan Lab · Reviewed 2026-09-24 · Research and planning, not product certification

What is a Japanese bidet seat?

An electric bidet seat replaces a toilet seat and adds a water-washing function. Depending on the model, it may heat water, warm the seat or offer a dryer. A non-electric attachment is a different product category with different requirements. WASHLET is TOTO’s trademark; “bidet seat” is the generic term used here.

TOTO dates WASHLET’s introduction to 1980 and reported cumulative worldwide sales of 70 million units in November 2025. That is a manufacturer-reported sales milestone across products and countries, not the number of Japanese households with a seat. The same release describes expanding overseas sales. It supports a long-established product category, not a claim that every Japanese home has one. [1]

Which problem does it solve?

It gives a household a washing option at an existing toilet without adding a separate bidet fixture. Whether that is useful depends on personal preference, the room and the effort needed to install and maintain it. Comfort features can be attractive, but additional electronics also bring cleaning tasks, energy use and eventual repair or replacement.

The Japanese example is useful because the equipment has evolved over decades. It does not establish that a Japanese domestic-market model is the right purchase abroad. A locally supported model, a simpler attachment or keeping the existing seat can each be a reasonable outcome.

Start with the toilet, not the feature list

Record the toilet manufacturer and model if visible. Measure from the mounting-bolt centre line to the front of the bowl, the bolt spacing and the space behind the bolts. Photograph the tank-to-bowl transition and the underside of the mounting holes. Compare this record with the chosen seat’s installation drawing.

TOTO’s fit chart separates models and bowl shapes and flags interfering contours. Brondell also publishes a dedicated fit guide. These are useful evidence that a general “round” or “elongated” description cannot settle every fit question. Our worksheet records the measurements; it does not turn one generic length threshold into a pass. [2, 3]

A practical stopping point: if the manufacturer cannot confirm the drawing against your toilet, resolve that question before ordering. Return costs and installed-product restrictions can make a speculative purchase inconvenient.

Electrical and plumbing checks come next

Compare the exact product’s voltage and frequency rating with the local supply. Confirm grounding, required protective devices, outlet placement, circuit capacity and the manufacturer’s cable-routing instructions with a qualified local installer. A plug that physically fits does not establish electrical compatibility.

On the water side, confirm the shut-off valve, thread and hose arrangement, permitted supply pressure and any local backflow-protection requirement. Follow the manual supplied for that model and market. The linked Brondell manual is a concrete example of product-specific installation conditions, not a substitute for another manufacturer’s instructions. [4]

Features worth comparing — and their trade-offs

QuestionWhy it affects the decision
Does the seat fit comfortably?The housing can change the usable seating space. Dimensions and a suitable return policy matter.
Can everyone use the controls?Reach, readable labels and a remote’s location may matter more than the number of settings.
Who will maintain it?Check cleaning access, filters, consumables and available service.
What happens without power?Confirm which functions remain available in the specific toilet/seat combination.
Is local support available?Imported domestic models may have unsuitable ratings, instructions or warranty coverage.

How much electricity will it use?

A nameplate’s rated power is not an average daily load. Water heating, seat settings, room temperature, standby behaviour and usage all affect consumption. Use a model’s declared energy figure with its test conditions, or a suitable measurement method approved for the installation. Annual running cost is annual measured kWh multiplied by the relevant electricity price. We do not apply a universal wattage to every seat.

Your buying sequence

  1. Choose the functions you actually need and a locally supported product range.
  2. Record the toilet’s dimensions and contour; compare the exact fit drawing.
  3. Get unresolved electrical and plumbing questions checked.
  4. Compare the full installed cost, maintenance and return terms.
  5. Keep the manual and model number for future cleaning and service.
