Heat-pump water heater installation: a pre-quote checklist

Collect the information an installer needs before comparing equipment prices.

By Everyday Japan Lab · Reviewed 2026-09-24 · Research and planning, not product certification

Identify the system layout first

Write down whether the proposed equipment has an indoor integrated heat pump or a separate outdoor unit. This determines which air, condensate, weather and noise questions need attention. A generic room-volume rule is not reliable across all models.

Record the site conditions

  • Access: doorway, stairs, installation footprint and room for future service or replacement.
  • Air and location: manufacturer-required airflow or ducting, permissible temperatures and any acoustic concerns.
  • Water and drainage: pipe connections, condensate routing, pressure-control arrangements and safe drainage as applicable.
  • Electrical supply: the exact rating, circuit requirements and local product approval.
  • Demand: household routines, simultaneous hot-water use and the required recovery performance.

The ENERGY STAR design and installation resources address integrated heat-pump water heaters and illustrate why airflow and placement belong in the decision. Use those resources within their intended product and market context. An installer must verify the actual model’s instructions. [1, 2]

Ask for an explanation of backup operation

A quote should identify the operating mode used for an energy estimate. Ask what happens during high demand or unfavourable conditions and whether resistance heating supplies part of the load. If that share is unknown, compare scenarios instead of assuming it is zero.

Keep the limits visible

This checklist does not select a tank capacity, certify a room, prescribe a temperature or approve electrical work. It organizes observations for a professional. Our calculator models annual energy; matching an annual total does not show that the heater can meet the busiest hour of the day.

A practical comparison includes the installed price, required alterations, expected service life information from the supplier, maintenance, warranty coverage and local repair availability. Record exclusions in each quote so that unlike packages are not compared as though they were identical.
