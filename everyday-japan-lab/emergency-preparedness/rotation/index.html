A practical pantry rotation and outage checklist

Build a reserve that is visible, usable and easy to replenish.

By Everyday Japan Lab · Reviewed 2026-09-24 · Research and planning, not product certification

Use a small inventory

A useful record needs an item name, quantity, location, the next date to check and whether preparation requires water, heat or electricity. A complicated inventory that nobody updates can be less useful than a short list kept near the cupboard.

MAFF’s rolling-stock guidance connects ordinary consumption with replenishment. Our suggested inventory is an editorial way to apply that idea, not a government-approved template. [1]

Keep quantity and usefulness separate

  • Count individual meals rather than assuming each package is a full meal.
  • Identify which items need cooking and whether the necessary safe preparation method is available.
  • Check household preferences and dietary requirements without treating the calculator as nutrition advice.
  • Place older appropriate stock in the normal use path and replace what is taken from the reserve.
  • Set a review routine that someone in the household will actually maintain.

Remember non-food supplies

Record sanitation products, lighting and other locally recommended household supplies separately. For toilet products, note supported uses per package, storage instructions and the local waste-disposal plan. Five uses per person per day is a planning input in the Japanese source, not a prediction of every individual’s needs. [2, 3]

A short review sheet

CheckRecord
Who is covered?People, days, pets and special supply requirements
What is usable now?Current quantities and storage condition
What is missing?Calculator deficits plus items outside the calculation
Who will replenish it?A named household task and review date
What changed?Season, household, local hazards or official advice

You can print the calculator with the chosen inputs and results. It saves nothing in an account or browser storage, so keep a copy if you need a record. Avoid writing personal health information into a public issue or feedback channel.

Sources and scope

Primary references checked 2026-09-24. Manufacturer statements are identified as such.

  1. MAFF Japan — Household food-stock guidance
    Rotate familiar foods; recommendation does not establish household compliance.
  2. Cabinet Office Japan — Public preparedness guidance
    Three days minimum and preferably a week; roughly 3 L drinking water per person per day. Guidance is not observed adoption.
  3. METI Japan — Disaster toilet working-group material
    Five uses per person per day is a planning assumption; individual and product needs vary.
